On planes and in trucks, space costs as much as weight. That is why carriers don't only look at kilos: they also work out the parcel's **volumetric weight** and charge whichever is higher.

## How it is calculated

Volumetric weight is the parcel's volume divided by a fixed number, the **divisor**:

**volumetric weight (kg) = length × width × height (cm) ÷ divisor**

The most common divisor is **6000**. Some lines use 5000 (stricter) or 8000 (more generous): the value is in the line's terms.

An example: a 40 × 30 × 20 cm box measures 24,000 cm³. With a 6000 divisor its volumetric weight is **4 kg**. If it only holds 1.5 kg of clothes, you still pay for 4 kg.

## Chargeable weight

The weight used for the price is the **chargeable weight**:

1. take the **higher** of actual and volumetric weight;
2. **round it up** to the line's weight step.

If a line has a 500 g first step and 500 g additional steps, a 1.2 kg parcel is charged as 1.5 kg. With 100 g steps it would be charged as 1.2 kg, which matters a lot for small parcels.

## How to cut volume

- **Remove original packaging** when you don't need it: shoe boxes are the classic case. Many agents do this on request, but think about whether you'll need it for a return.
- **Vacuum bags** for clothes and soft textiles.
- **Ship together** items that fit well, rather than many small parcels.
- **Protect what is fragile**: saving a few centimetres isn't worth a broken item.
- For light, bulky parcels, look for **lines with a high divisor** or no volumetric charge below a threshold.
